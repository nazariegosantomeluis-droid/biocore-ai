# BIOCORE AI

# GLOSSARY

Version: 1.0

Status: Active

Classification: Reference Document

---

# Purpose

This glossary defines the official terminology used throughout BIOCORE AI.

Every engineering document, source code, AI prompt, API specification, research paper and user interface should adopt these definitions.

Using consistent terminology is essential for maintaining architectural coherence.

---

# A

## AI Agent

A specialized artificial intelligence component responsible for a single biomedical or engineering task.

Examples include:

Clinical AI

Educational AI

Research AI

Simulation AI

Prediction AI

Voice AI

Vision AI

Gesture AI

---

## AI Orchestrator

The central intelligence coordinator responsible for selecting, routing and validating specialized AI agents.

The AI Orchestrator never performs biomedical reasoning directly.

Instead, it delegates work to specialized agents.

---

# B

## Biomedical Operating System

The official definition of BIOCORE AI.

A software ecosystem capable of integrating biomedical sensing, multimodal artificial intelligence, digital physiological simulation, education and research into one computational environment.

BIOCORE AI shall always be referred to internally as a Biomedical Operating System.

---

## Biomedical Pipeline

A computational workflow that converts raw physiological signals into validated physiological descriptors.

Example

Raw ECG

↓

Filtering

↓

R Peak Detection

↓

HRV

↓

Features

---

# C

## Clinical Intelligence

The collection of computational services that assist clinicians and students in interpreting physiological information.

Clinical Intelligence includes:

Risk estimation

Interpretation

Recommendations

Explainability

Decision support

---

# D

## Digital Human Twin

The computational representation of a living human organism.

It visualizes the Unified Physiological State.

The Digital Twin is not a simulation engine.

It is the visual interface of physiology.

---

## Digital Organ

An independent physiological subsystem inside the Digital Twin.

Examples:

Heart

Brain

Lungs

Muscles

Blood

Autonomic Nervous System

---

# E

## Educational Engine

The subsystem responsible for adaptive biomedical learning.

Includes:

Interactive laboratories

Virtual patients

Adaptive quizzes

Learning analytics

Competency tracking

---

# F

## Feature Extraction

The computational process that transforms physiological signals into measurable descriptors.

Examples:

Heart Rate

RR Interval

RMSSD

Alpha Power

EMG RMS

Respiratory Rate

---

## Fusion Engine

The subsystem responsible for combining multiple physiological systems into one unified organism representation.

This is one of BIOCORE AI's core innovations.

---

# G

## Gesture Interface

A natural interaction system based on computer vision.

Allows users to manipulate the platform using hand movements.

---

# H

## Hardware Layer

The architectural layer responsible for communication with biomedical acquisition devices.

Examples:

ESP32

BLE

USB

Wearables

IoMT Devices

---

# I

## Intelligent Workflow

A workflow enhanced by AI reasoning, prediction or educational guidance.

---

# M

## Multimodal Physiology

The simultaneous analysis of multiple physiological systems.

Example

ECG

+

EEG

+

EMG

+

Respiration

+

SpO₂

↓

Integrated physiological understanding.

---

# P

## Physiological Descriptor

A measurable characteristic extracted from physiological signals.

Examples:

Heart Rate

HRV

EEG Alpha Power

EMG RMS

SpO₂

Respiratory Variability

---

## Physiological Fusion

The process of integrating descriptors from multiple physiological systems.

---

## Physiological Intelligence

The ability to understand relationships between physiological systems rather than isolated biomedical signals.

---

## Physiological State

The internal computational representation of one physiological subsystem.

Example

Cardiovascular State

Neurological State

Respiratory State

---

# R

## Research Engine

The subsystem responsible for scientific experimentation, reproducibility and biomedical benchmarking.

---

# S

## Signal Processing

The computational transformation of raw biomedical signals.

Includes

Filtering

Normalization

Artifact Removal

Feature Extraction

Spectral Analysis

---

# U

## Unified Physiological State (UPS)

The single source of truth representing the current physiological condition of an organism.

Every biomedical module contributes to the UPS.

Every visualization consumes the UPS.

No module should maintain an independent physiological state.

---

# V

## Virtual Patient

A computational patient generated for biomedical education and simulation.

Virtual Patients can evolve over time and respond to physiological interventions.

---

# W

## Wearable

A biomedical acquisition device capable of continuously monitoring physiological signals.

Examples

Smartwatches

Smart ECG devices

Pulse oximeters

Portable EEG

---

# X

## Explainable AI (XAI)

Artificial Intelligence capable of exposing the reasoning behind every biomedical conclusion.

Every AI subsystem inside BIOCORE AI should support Explainability whenever technically possible.

---

# Official Naming Convention

BIOCORE AI

Never BioCore

Never Biocore

Never Biomedical Signal Visualizer (legacy project name)

The official platform name is:

BIOCORE AI

The official abbreviation is:

BCAI
